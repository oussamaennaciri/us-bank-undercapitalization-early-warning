  \slidelabel{Data-fit note: FDIC quarterly data vs.\ my question}

  \begin{columns}[t]
    \begin{column}{0.47\textwidth}
      \blocklabel{Provenance}
      {\fontsize{8.4}{10.4}\selectfont The data comes directly from the FDIC's public BankFind Suite API, the primary source behind all FDIC bank data products.}\par\vspace{5pt}

      \blocklabel{Fit: can answer}
      {\fontsize{8.4}{10.4}\selectfont The question directly: the dataset contains the exact capital ratios regulators use to classify a bank as undercapitalized (the PCA ratios).}\par\vspace{5pt}

      \blocklabel{Fit: can't answer}
      {\fontsize{8.4}{10.4}\selectfont Anything that happens faster than a quarter, and anything banks omit or don't report, such as confidential supervisory information or fraud.}
    \end{column}
    \begin{column}{0.47\textwidth}
      \blocklabel{Three limitations}
      {\fontsize{8.4}{10.4}\selectfont
      \begin{enumerate}\itemsep=2pt
        \item \textbf{Quarterly lag}: snapshots every three months; fast collapses like SVB's 48-hour run happen between filings. The model catches slow slides, not overnight runs.
        \item \textbf{Rare target}: becoming undercapitalized is uncommon, so classes are severely imbalanced; the evaluation metric must be chosen for rare events.
        \item \textbf{Self-reported data}: call reports are filed by the banks themselves; a bank concealing problems can look healthy until it isn't.
      \end{enumerate}}
    \end{column}
  \end{columns}
  \vspace{2pt}
  \textcolor{hairline}{\rule{\textwidth}{0.5pt}}\par\vspace{2pt}
  {\sffamily\bfseries\fontsize{8.5}{11}\selectfont\color{navy}DECISION\quad}{\fontsize{8.6}{10.6}\selectfont \textbf{Keep.} Quality data from a reliable source that answers the question as asked.}
